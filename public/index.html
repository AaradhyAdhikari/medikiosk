Why MediKiosk

Finally, a history the doctor can trust — not a form the patient guessed at.

Hospital registration captures a name and an age. Health apps assume a smartphone and a login. Neither reads the crumpled prescription in the patient's hand. MediKiosk does both, in the patient's language, and reads it back to them before the doctor sees a word.

01

Speak or tap, in seven languages

Hindi, English, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Tamil and Telugu — the whole interview, every option read aloud. Follow-up questions are written for the complaint, the way a physician would ask them.

02

The paper they already carry

Old prescriptions, lab reports and discharge summaries are photographed at the kiosk and read line by line — diagnoses, medicines with doses, values against their reference ranges, in date order.

03

Read back, confirmed, then verified

The patient hears their own history in their language and confirms it. The doctor accepts or amends each line, is warned about drug interactions as they prescribe, and that becomes the next visit's baseline.

How a visit works

Six steps, and the patient does the first five before they sit down.

1

Choose a language

Seven scripts on the first screen. From here on every word, prompt and option is in that language, and read aloud on request.

Patient · kiosk
2

Say who you are

Mobile number, ABHA number or Aadhaar — a one-time code proves the phone. Returning patients are recognised; new ones get an ABHA and, if they like, a login for their records.

Patient · kiosk
3

Answer by voice or by tap

Chief complaint, then follow-up questions written for that complaint. Point to where it hurts on a body map. An Ayurvedic history — Prakriti, Agni, Koshtha — if the hospital asks for it.

Patient · kiosk
4

Scan the papers you brought

Hold each report or prescription to the camera. It is read, dated and filed into a timeline; abnormal values are flagged before anyone looks.

Patient · kiosk
5

Hear it back, take your token

The history is read back in the patient's language. They confirm it, choose what the doctor may see, and get a token slip with a QR code and a department.

Patient · kiosk
6

The doctor verifies and prescribes

The structured history is already on the console, sources beside every line. The doctor accepts or amends, sees interaction warnings while prescribing, and pushes a FHIR record to the patient's ABHA locker.

Built

Voice, both ways

A mic on every open question and spoken next / back / repeat. Every screen reads itself aloud, in all seven languages, on any machine.

Built

Ayurveda history mode

Dashavidha Pariksha, Agni, Koshtha, Ahara-Vihara — all ten parameters, Vikriti kept apart from Prakriti.

Built

Red-flag triage

An emergency button or a red-flag answer: the token turns priority, the case tops the queue, the console sounds, and staff get an SMS and email.

Built

Reads the paper

Printed and handwritten prescriptions, lab reports, discharge summaries — read, extracted, dated into a timeline, abnormal values in red.

Built

Drug interaction check

Scanned papers, reported medicines and today's prescription as it is typed — curated rules for allopathic and Ayurvedic pairs.

Built

Consent first, revocable

Four separate permissions, read aloud. "Withdraw consent" on the patient's dashboard locks every clinician out of that visit.

Built

FHIR R4 record

A real document bundle — Composition, Condition with ICD-10 and NAMASTE codes, Observations, Consent — downloadable from the console. The push awaits ABDM sandbox access.

Questions

The things judges and clinicians ask first.

Is MediKiosk a medical device? Does it diagnose?

No. It takes a history and organises documents. Every clinical decision is the physician's; the console shows a draft to accept, amend or reject, and records who did which. It is a Smart India Hackathon prototype and not a live service.

Where does the patient's data go?

Voice never leaves the kiosk as audio — recognition runs in the browser and only text is kept. Document and summary text go to a model API over TLS. In a hospital deployment the same code points at a hospital-hosted model, so nothing leaves the building. Consent is granular and revocable from the patient's own dashboard.

Which languages, and how deep does it go?

Hindi, English, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Tamil and Telugu — the whole interview: every question, option, consent line and error message, plus spoken prompts. The physician's summary is always in English; the read-back to the patient is in their language.

Does it work when the Wi-Fi drops?

The kiosk shell — pages, scripts, language packs — is cached, so the screen never goes blank. Anything that needs the server (codes, summaries, the queue) waits for the network and says so, rather than showing stale data.

Are the ABHA numbers real?

Not yet. They are validated by format and generated locally; issuing real ones needs ABDM sandbox credentials, which are applied for. The FHIR R4 bundle that would be pushed to the ABHA locker is real and downloadable today — only the transport is mocked.

What if the patient cannot read, or cannot see well?

Audio-guided mode reads every screen, heading, hint and option aloud, and accepts spoken answers and spoken navigation. Large-text and high-contrast switches are on the accessibility screen. A screen left alone clears itself within two minutes.

Ready to see a visit run end to end?

Three minutes as a patient, then open the console as the doctor. The example cases load from the console if you want a full queue.

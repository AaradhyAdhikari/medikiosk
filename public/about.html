How MediKiosk works

SIH26047 · Ministry of Ayush · All India Institute of Ayurveda

The loop

A patient answers at the kiosk by speaking or tapping and scans the paper they already carry. The system assembles a structured history and puts it on the doctor's screen before the patient sits down. The doctor verifies it, amends anything wrong, and prescribes — and that becomes the baseline the next follow-up interview starts from.

The token slip

The patient leaves with a token, a department and a QR code, and can print it. Scanning the code opens that patient's case on the consultation console, so the registration desk scans instead of typing — check-in is where an OPD queue actually stalls. The code is eight characters rather than a database id, so the symbol stays coarse enough to scan off a thermal print, and it is printed as text as well in case the QR is smudged. The encoder is written into the app — no library, no CDN — because a kiosk may be on a network that cannot reach one, and it was checked against a reference implementation and by decoding the printed slip back, not by eye.

Seven languages, the whole interview

Hindi, English, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Tamil and Telugu — not the buttons, the whole interview: every question, every option, every consent line. A language appears on the first screen only when its translation file is actually complete, and a string nobody has translated shows English rather than Hindi, because a Tamil reader cannot tell a Hindi sentence from a bug. Sanskrit terms — Prakriti, Vikriti, Agni, Koshtha — are transliterated into the reader's script rather than translated, because a physician uses them by name. Red-flag detection and department routing follow the languages too: the routing keywords are derived from the translation files themselves, so a phrase corrected by a native speaker corrects the router at the same time. These translations were produced with machine assistance and have not yet been checked by native speakers — a review sheet per language is generated for exactly that, and it should be done before any pilot.

Where the patient is sent

A hospital is a set of departments, so an intake that cannot name one has only moved the queue rather than shortened it. Every completed interview is routed to exactly one clinic — a keyword router runs on every intake and never fails, and the AI summary may overrule it where it has better reason. A clinician registers as a specialist or as a general clinician, and a specialist's queue is their own department, so the dentist opens the console and sees dental cases. Three things are never filtered away: anything flagged urgent, anything not yet routed, and nothing at all for a general clinician. A specialist can still lift the filter to cover a colleague, and that view is written to the audit trail. The routing is a suggestion — any clinician can reassign a patient to another department, and who moved them and why stays on the record.

Real in this build

Three ways to identify — mobile, ABHA number, or Aadhaar with a real Verhoeff checksum · a personal login ID and password layered on top of the code, so a patient can return to their own dashboard of past visits, documents and summaries, read-only · adaptive question flow with first-visit, follow-up, proxy and emergency branching · voice input, spoken prompts and spoken navigation commands in every language, with a runtime check that says so when a machine has no voice for one · large-text and high-contrast accessibility modes · a literacy-free body map · all ten Dashavidha Pariksha parameters with Vikriti separated from Prakriti, plus Agni, Koshtha and Ahara-Vihara · document capture with AI extraction of diagnoses, medicines and lab values · a chronological document timeline built from the date printed on each paper, with anything unreadable marked undated rather than guessed into position · AI clinical summary with assessment, differentials and suggested investigations · ICD-10 and NAMASTE coding suggestions · rule-based red-flag triage · real SMS delivery when a gateway is configured · clinician registration with HPR ID and approval gating · live queue · automatic department routing with per-department queues, an audited override and clinician reassignment · single-action history verification with amend · prescription write-back · granular DPDP consent · full audit trail.

Mocked, and why

ABHA numbers are generated locally in the correct format, and the FHIR push to a hospital HIS is built but not transmitted, because ABDM sandbox credentials require an application and an approval period. Speech recognition in all seven languages runs on the browser's own Web Speech API, which is Chrome-only; a deployment would use Bhashini, and that needs a key. Everything else — including SMS — is real once configured.

Why this is not what already exists

Hospital registration systems capture demographics only. Health apps assume a smartphone, literacy and enrolment before the visit. Nurse-led history desks do not scale to five thousand patients a day. None of them digitise the paper the patient is already holding. This does both — deep structured history and document digitisation — before the encounter begins.

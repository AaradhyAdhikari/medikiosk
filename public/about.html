How MediKiosk works

SIH26047 · Ministry of Ayush · All India Institute of Ayurveda

The loop

A patient answers at the kiosk by speaking or tapping and scans the paper they already carry. The system assembles a structured history and puts it on the doctor's screen before the patient sits down. The doctor verifies it, amends anything wrong, and prescribes — and that becomes the baseline the next follow-up interview starts from.

Real in this build

Three ways to identify — mobile, ABHA number, or Aadhaar with a real Verhoeff checksum · a personal login ID and password layered on top of the code, so a patient can return to their own dashboard of past visits, documents and summaries, read-only · adaptive question flow with first-visit, follow-up, proxy and emergency branching · voice input, spoken prompts and spoken navigation commands · large-text and high-contrast accessibility modes · a literacy-free body map · all ten Dashavidha Pariksha parameters with Vikriti separated from Prakriti, plus Agni, Koshtha and Ahara-Vihara · document capture with AI extraction of diagnoses, medicines and lab values · a chronological document timeline built from the date printed on each paper, with anything unreadable marked undated rather than guessed into position · AI clinical summary with assessment, differentials and suggested investigations · ICD-10 and NAMASTE coding suggestions · rule-based red-flag triage · real SMS delivery when a gateway is configured · clinician registration with HPR ID and approval gating · live queue · single-action history verification with amend · prescription write-back · granular DPDP consent · full audit trail.

Mocked, and why

ABHA numbers are generated locally in the correct format, and the FHIR push to a hospital HIS is built but not transmitted, because ABDM sandbox credentials require an application and an approval period. Multilingual ASR beyond Hindi and English needs a Bhashini key. Everything else — including SMS — is real once configured.

Why this is not what already exists

Hospital registration systems capture demographics only. Health apps assume a smartphone, literacy and enrolment before the visit. Nurse-led history desks do not scale to five thousand patients a day. None of them digitise the paper the patient is already holding. This does both — deep structured history and document digitisation — before the encounter begins.
